Cross-Cutting Ways the Lean Formalizations Differ from Published Literature

  1. Bespoke global CM host restrictions (D7, Host E, HSp D): Dozens of Siegel–Weil, toroidal-compactification, trace-formula, and automorphic-form specs are not stated for general number fields or even arbitrary CM fields. Host E forces $E$ to contain $\sqrt{-d}$ for a prime $d \equiv 7 \pmod{16}$ with $E/E^+$ unramified everywhere, uniform archimedean signature $(p, q)$, and a unimodular alternating lattice; D : D7 forces $E$ to be a specific Galois abelian CM field inside $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{Q_S q d})$, everywhere unramified over $E^+$, with V : HSp D requiring a globally unimodular self-dual $\mathcal{O}_E$-lattice.
  2. Pervasive domain/characteristic/prime restrictions: Global $\mathrm{GL}_n$ automorphic theorems are mostly restricted to [IsTotallyComplex E] (or split into totally complex vs. totally real); $p$-adic $\mathrm{GL}_n$ and LLC theorems are restricted to [CharZero k] / completions of number fields; Green and depth-zero inertia theorems are restricted to prime fields $\mathbb{F}_g$ / $\mathbb{Q}_g$; several geometric/Shimura statements are restricted to characteristic $0$ or affine bases.
  3. Weakened conclusions and omitted equivalences: Many categorical equivalences (prismatic Dieudonné, Bhatt–Scholze, Fontaine, Mumford degeneration, Casselman–Wallach, GAGA, Honda–Tate, Zarhin) are weakened to one-sided existence, drop essential surjectivity/full faithfulness/uniqueness/canonicality, or drop functoriality (e.g., KLocalisation and KDwyerFriedlander assert bare existence of some homomorphisms; PoitouTate* uses existential connecting maps; NadelVanishingPair omits identifying the ideal sheaf as $\mathcal{J}(X, \Delta)$).
  4. Rare strengthenings or missing axioms relative to literature: A few specs are stronger than the cited source (e.g., ShinGaloisRep includes full monodromy compatibility away from $\ell$ without Caraiani's extra hypotheses; CasselmanWallach omits admissibility/finite-length on the Fréchet target E; TaylorYoshidaSquareIntegrable drops the imaginary quadratic subfield hypothesis), while LocalConstantsExist / DeligneLocalConstants omit the monodromy-$N$ axiom of Deligne (1973) §8.12 completely.

Methodology: Identifying Collapsible Existing-Theory Proof Subtrees

A naive directory-based filter fails to isolate existing-theory proofs in this repository: the four PublishedTheorems folders comprise only 469 modules (0.8%) and consist almost entirely of def ... : Prop specifications rather than proofs, while the actual proofs live downstream across Development/, GroundTheory/, CompanionTheory/, and PositivityTheory/ and are wired into the main argument at discharge hubs (EveryCharacterOfLiteratureOfVWin.lean, TopMainZj.lean, PublishedHypothesesFromPatching.lean, TopCovolSlim2Use.lean, MainConclusion.lean). Conversely, ~14,024 foundational modules (Formalism/, ReciprocityTheory/, Definitions/) are needed merely to state the interfaces and must remain visible.

To identify only the subtrees that exist to prove already-existing peer-reviewed theory, the DAG pipeline derives a deterministic published-theorem proof-frontier cut directly from Sections 1–12 of this inventory (90 active literature theorem groups matching reachable modules out of 109 cataloged):

  1. Inventory-Driven Target Set & Section 12 Exclusion: Parse every backticked Lean Prop identifier from Sections 1–11 (and its bold canonical literature title in Column 2), while explicitly excluding all symbols cataloged in Section 12 (project-packaged analytic NT bounds, project-tailored constants, internal glue predicates, scaffolding A0..KY, and sanity checks). Restricting targets strictly to this audited inventory prevents generic Mathlib identifiers (such as IsIntegral) from matching unrelated algebraic lemmas.
  2. Defining-Module Ancestor Guard: Using graph.sqlite, resolve each target symbol to its defining Lean module(s) in PublishedTheorems or the companion hubs in Section 11, and compute the set of transitive importers (ancestors). A theorem/lemma is only accepted as a proof of symbol S if its module transitively imports a defining module of S.
  3. Final-Conclusion Proof-Root Detection & Consumer/Bundle Guards: Parse the signature of every theorem and lemma in candidate modules using balanced-delimiter scanning to extract the final conclusion head(s) after the last top-level → (splitting top-level ∧ conjuncts). This separates provers (theorem foo : GlobalTheory.Published.Bar) from consumers (theorem baz (h : GlobalTheory.Published.Bar) : NovelResult). To avoid false positives:
    • Exclude any module under */Definitions/*.
    • Exclude any theorem whose premises take an assumed literature hypothesis bundle (HypothesesU, Hypotheses2, HypothesesUE, LitN, LitLoc*, LitAut, LitCoh, LitCM, PublishedHodgeBundleTheorems, ThetaLocalInputs, LiteratureRankNHypothesis, OpenImageCondition, OpenLiteratureStatements, etc.), which merely projects or dispatches fields out of an assumed bundle.
    • Exclude any module that also proves a novel headline theorem (RiemannHypothesis, EveryCharacter, VWin, CorGK, Bound, CovolX, CovolumeBoundOfX, DeepStR, GoodTowerExistsX, VolumeTheoremX, etc.).
    • Iteratively peel multi-topic re-export barrels: any candidate proof root whose proven literature conclusions span $\ge 2$ inventory rows and whose direct imports already include candidate proof roots spanning $\ge 2$ inventory rows is removed from the cut set so each theorem subtree retains its own Literature Supernode rather than merging behind a forwarding hub.
  4. Targeted Dead Transitive Import Pruning on Split Bridges: Checked automatically when standalone split-bridge modules (such as RiemannHypothesisOfEveryCharacter.lean) are present; no split-bridge dead-import pruning is needed in this clumped/simplified codebase.
  5. Frontier-Domination Cut & Literature-Supernode Contraction: Run reachability from Main without traversing outgoing edges of the detected proof roots $B$. Every node still reached is either active (novel proof or shared definition/contract) or a frontier proof root $f \in F \subseteq B$; every unreachable module forms the collapsed proof-only interior $C$ (strictly dominated by $B$). Grouping $F$ by its inventory row in Sections 1–11 into axiom-sink Literature Supernodes $S_T$ (out_degree = 0) and partitioning $C$ via deterministic multi-source BFS assigns every collapsed module to a unique supernode while guaranteeing strict DAG acyclicity and exact conservation of module counts.
